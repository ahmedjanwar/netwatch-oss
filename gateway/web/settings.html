Give this machine a fixed IP address

Right now this machine gets its address () from your router. Once your router stops handing out addresses in step 2, this machine would lose it, so you fix it first.

On this machine (or over SSH), run:

You should see Static IP active and internet works. The address doesn't change, so an SSH session stays connected.

2

Turn off DHCP and IPv6 on your router

Open your router's admin page at and sign in (the default login is usually printed on the sticker under the router). Menu names differ between routers, but look for:

  1. Local Network (sometimes LAN or Home Network) → DHCP server → set it to Off/Disabled → Save.
  2. Under IPv6, turn off IPv6 on the LAN completely ("IPv6 state" → Off). Turning off only the "DHCPv6 server" is not enough: most routers also hand out their DNS in Router Advertisements. While that's on, phones and laptops use your internet provider's DNS over IPv6 and skip Netwatch and the ad blocker.

Nothing breaks yet: devices keep their current addresses until their lease runs out, which takes hours.

4

Move your devices over

Each device switches the next time it renews its address. To make that happen now:

  • Phones, tablets, laptops: turn Wi-Fi off and back on.
  • TV, PlayStation, PC: restart them, or unplug for 10 seconds.
  • Smart bulbs and plugs: they switch over by themselves within an hour. Flick them off and on at the wall to speed it up.

On the dashboard, a device's ring turns from dashed to solid once it's routed.

Undo everything

If the internet stops working, or you just want to go back:

  1. Your router's admin page: turn its DHCP server back on.
  2. Turn gateway mode off:
  3. Turn Wi-Fi off and on on your devices.

If the dashboard itself won't load, run sudo netwatch gateway off on this machine. Its fixed IP can stay as it is; it's harmless (or undo it with sudo netwatch static-ip revert).

Good to know

  • This machine is now critical. If it's off, devices lose internet until you turn your router's DHCP back on. Keep it plugged in (on a laptop, set closing the lid to "do nothing").
  • Blocking cuts a device's internet but keeps it on your home network. A tech-savvy user could get around it by manually setting their gateway to your router.
  • Port forwarding set up on your router won't work for devices behind Netwatch.
  • Casting and AirPlay go directly between devices, so that traffic doesn't show on the dashboard.
  • Addresses are leased for 1 hour, so changes and rollbacks take effect quickly.

Forgot your password?

On the machine running Netwatch (or over SSH), run:

sudo netwatch reset-password

It prints a new temporary password. Sign in with it and you'll be asked to choose a new one.
